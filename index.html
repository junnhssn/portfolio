MorphicCase Study

Communication and narrative-building for a generative AI video startup.

300+creators researched
50+creatorCollaborations
closed
1M+impressions across the launch

The role

I joined Morphic as a Communication & Narrative Operator, working across product marketing, brand communication, creator partnerships, and written content. My role sat at the intersection of strategy and execution: shaping how we talked about the product, building narratives around launches and campaigns, and getting those stories in front of the right audiences.

Product marketing & launch

The Workflows launch ran across the founder’s personal account, the brand’s social channels, LinkedIn, and email: one narrative, retuned for each place it landed.

Creator partnerships

01 | HOW I FOUND THE RIGHT CREATORS

I researched 300+ creators, looking at average views, posting consistency, audience demographics, country, and overall audience fit. I used creator analytics tools to understand whether someone had a genuinely engaged audience rather than just a big follower count.

02 | WHAT MADE THE OUTREACH WORK

We offered creators a combination of affiliate partnerships and paid collaborations. My outreach was personalised: I referenced 2 to 3 of their own videos, shared relevant reference videos, and showed them how Morphic’s features could help them create something similar. Creators responded because they could tell we had actually looked at their work rather than sending them a mass pitch.

03 | A COLLABORATION THAT STOOD OUT

One collaboration I particularly enjoyed was with @cyborggirll, an Indian creator making some really interesting work. We had a clear understanding of what we wanted to create together, which made the collaboration feel natural from the start. I especially enjoyed being able to work with a creator whose style and ideas felt fresh and different.

What I’d do differently

I would have built a more structured way to track creator performance and used those insights more consistently to shape future briefs. That would have helped us understand which features and creative approaches were landing with audiences, and make a stronger case for what creators should show.

HeadoutCase Study

SEO and landing page copy for a global experiences marketplace.

What I did at Headout

I turned search queries into landing pages people could use. I researched keywords and search intent, figured out what information travellers were looking for, and used that to structure and write SEO-focused pages for experiences including the Grévin Museum and Agafay Desert.

The brief

What was Headout trying to achieve?

Headout wanted to improve its organic visibility for high-intent searches around experiences and attractions, creating landing pages that could rank well while giving potential customers the information they needed to make a booking.

What was my remit?

I handled SEO end to end across the Italy and France markets, covering keyword research, search intent, page structure, and landing page copy. I worked across the full page, from deciding what to target and how to structure it to writing the final copy.

How I worked

01 | HOW I PICKED THE KEYWORDS

I used tools like Keywords Everywhere and Google Search Console to research search volume and identify keywords with strong potential. I also looked at worldwide search data to understand what people were searching for across markets, rather than limiting the research to a single country.

02 | HOW I STRUCTURED THE PAGE

I started with user intent. For a wax museum, for example, I thought about what someone would want to know before visiting, from how the experience compares to Madame Tussauds to practical details like timings. Headout provided a base template, but I customised the structure and content around each individual experience.

03 | WHAT I LEARNED ABOUT SEARCH INTENT

SEO taught me to get into the head of the customer and think beyond the obvious keywords. People search for everything from major questions to tiny practical details like timings, but those details can make or break a trip. Good SEO is ultimately about understanding what someone needs to know before they even know they need to ask.

Results

Page I worked on

At Headout, I optimized 10+ landing and shoulder pages, rewriting for accuracy and readability and refining keywords with Keywords Everywhere and Google Search Console. Several of these pages now appear on Google for their target searches (screenshots above), and supply partners highlighted the revamped content for clarity and quality. Internal traffic and conversion data is confidential, so I’m sharing only what’s publicly visible.

YAAS MediaCase Study

Social and video storytelling for a media brand.

The brief

YAAS Media is a media agency that creates ads and IPs for brands through short-form, fully organic content on Instagram. I joined in January 2026 as a presenter and the face of The Career Hook, and stayed through April. Because I was in front of the camera, I got pulled into ideation and scripting early on, and my role grew from there.

The work

I made skits, talking-head videos, and a series on how to handle interview situations. I acted in every format, and over time I started suggesting changes to scripts and helped shape the writing for The Career Hook.

What I’m proudest of from YAAS is what I learned by being involved from pre-production to post-production: how a shoot actually runs, what it takes to present well on camera, and the challenges that come with it. Working closely with the team made me a better writer, because I started writing for how a line would be performed and filmed as well as how it reads on the page.

Social · Community · Content · Events

DecodingDraupadi

Social, community, and events for a platform on women’s health and money.

The brief

Decoding Draupadi is a community for working women in India, built around four pillars: health, money, work, and relationships. I joined as a Creative Generalist from May to August 2024, while I was still in college. The company was at a very early stage, so the role was wide: I ran community engagement, helped host events, handled Instagram and the blog, helped with the podcast, and designed the visuals myself.

Social & content

I wrote and designed everything on this page myself, and design is where I learned the most. Making content for a community that is also an audience taught me to think about one person scrolling past, not a market. It was an early-stage page, so I measured it by what I could see: daily posting and replying grew the Instagram following by 50% in a month.

Community & events

I ran a women’s-only open mic in Pune, where about 25 people showed up. Between events, we kept the community together through a WhatsApp group, replies in the comments, and a recurring series, so the page felt like a conversation rather than a broadcast.

What I took from it

I went in focused on what women want, and ended up changing my own perspective. It’s why I’m drawn to work in women’s spaces, and I’m still grateful for the connections I made there.
